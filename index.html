Chapter 05

The 80% endpoint (Lytx)

At Lytx (video telematics, through GlobalLogic), clients pulled data one record per call. I designed a bulk endpoint: one batch request, handed to a new SQL Server stored procedure that processes the whole set inside the database. Client processing time dropped 80%.

Most performance wins are about the shape of the API.

Chapter 07

A team of agents, one human

At Coalfire, in May 2026, I designed a five-agent pipeline. Transcript agent: meeting to specs. Backlog agent: specs to tasks. Developer agent: opens the PR. QA agent: tests it with Playwright. Tech-lead agent: reviews it. Any failed check sends the work back.

Agents do the work. I keep the calls you can't undo.

Chapter 08

Now: ArggonManager

My last project at GlobalLogic wrapped in August 2026. Since then I've been building ArggonManager, my own methodology for AI agents. Specs and acceptance criteria live in the repo, agents claim, build, review and verify the work, and there's a done gate they can't skip. Built with it: dotnet-batchyard, C# microservices.

The irreversible calls stay human.

my methodology for AI agents · TypeScript

ArggonManager

A work methodology for AI agents that lives in git. Four agent roles: delivery lead, standards reviewer, maker and verifier. Work is sized by risk, from a plain task up to spec, exploration and ADR. Reviews leave evidence on the item, and nothing closes with an unticked acceptance criterion. Waivers, claim takeovers, reopening and releases are human-only, and the tool enforces it. Runs on OpenCode, ZCode and Claude Code. 27 ADRs. This site is tracked with it.

showcase · C# · .NET 10

dotnet-batchyard

An open-source .NET 10 backend showcase: batch ingest, JWT plans and roles, CQRS payments with an outbox and RabbitMQ, and a strangler migration from a monolith to a service behind YARP, traced with OpenTelemetry. In k6 tests on a cloud VM at 500 items per request, median latency fell 93% (about 300 ms to 20 ms) and throughput rose from about 7.7k to 95k items/s.
